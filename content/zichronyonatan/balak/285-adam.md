This week’s Parsha contains within it one of the deepest missing pieces in our generation’s treatment of one another.

Let me give you a brief overview of the events:

King Balak calls Bilam to curse the Israelite nation. After a brief back and forth between Bilam and G-d, and then Bilam and Balak by way of messengers and emissaries he agrees to come on the condition (supplied by G-d) that he will only say the words Hashem tells him to.

Bilam arises and saddles his donkey and begins his journey. Shortly thereafter, the donkey stops midway down the path and chooses an off-road route for she can see a fiery angel of “G-d guarding the way. Bilam gives her a kick to set her straight and she returns to the path only to flee the other direction, this time pinning his leg to a wall. Bilam beats her again.

Now the angel stands directly in the middle of the path blocking the entire way through causing the donkey to lay down mid-journey. Furious, Bilam strikes a third time and immediately G-d allows the donkey speech, an open miracle.

““What have I done to you that you have beaten me these three times?” asks the donkey.

“You have made a mockery of me! If I had a sword with me, I’d kill you,” Bilam replies.

“Am I not the ass that you have been riding all along until this day! Have I been in the habit of doing this to you?”

“No.”

At this point G-d allows Bilam, too, to see the angel and he bows deep until the ground.

The angel then admonishes Bilam, “Why have you beaten your donkey? It was because of me that she shied away these three times. And had she not, it would have been you that I’d killed and allowed her to live.”

These are the verses (very slightly paraphrased).

Rashi on this final verse allows us to understand the meaning of that last statement: and allowed her to live: But now that she admonished you and you had no response to defend yourself, I have killed the donkey lest onlookers later say, “That’s the donkey that admonished Bilam.” Such is the regard G-d has for human dignity.

Many point out how far this regard goes: Despite all possible gains to be had from others experiencing this open miracle of a talking donkey, in order to save the honor of one of the most morally decrepit individuals of the time, G-d takes the life of the donkey.

I think we can learn immensely from meditation on this point. Anyone we are coming across is surely not as big a villain as Bilam and surely has nowhere near the evil intent behind anything they say or do.

We have an opportunity to emulate G-d, to open the possibility for others to save face, to remove incriminating statements from the public view, and to show our regard for the sheer humanity of those we encounter despite even their obvious errors in action, judgement, or approach.

How great are the tents of Jacob?!

I love you all and miss you!
