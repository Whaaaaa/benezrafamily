Pesach is almost upon us and we're all running around like chickens without heads, frantically trying to get everything done in time. As we work so hard (and spend so much money) to celebrate Passover properly, it behooves us to tap into its power and take all that we can out of it.

I'd like to share a quick thought from Rabbi Dessler (1892-1953), of which Uncle Yonatan was very fond. Rabbi Dessler says that, in a spiritual sense, time does not flow as a line but rather as a circle, going around each year. He explains that each chag (holiday) has the same power as the original event had, so many years ago. Hence, Rosh Hashana is intrinsically a time of renewal, Shavuot a time of connection to Torah, Pesach a time of freedom, and so on. During these times we are able to tap into their incredible forces and experience similar events in our own lives.

Hence Pesach, the time of our freedom, is a time when we can be set free from whatever shackles may bind us. If we are in bondage to bad habits and poor decisions of the past, we can break free and turn over a new leaf. If we are stuck in a rut in our personal or professional lives, then we can move on this Pesach.

This is an intrinsic element which lies within these days, and we need only open our eyes to see its impact on our world. That said, it is in our hands to decide how much we will allow ourselves to be changed by it. The more we connect to the lessons of Pesach and to the true meaning of freedom, the more we will be able to take out of the chag. This means learning the lesson that true freedom is not the ability to do anything we wish but rather the ability to rein in our desires and take control of our lives.

So this Pesach, as we sit like royalty at our seder tables, let us understand the power of the chag. Let us see it's repercussions in the world around us. And let us become free people, free of our own limitations and able to choose our own direction in life.

Chag Sameach,

Yosef
