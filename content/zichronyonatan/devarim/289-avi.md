On the first day of the month of Shevat, five weeks before Moshe’s passing on, the nation is assembled before their elderly judge and leader. Moshe begins the repetition of the Torah and gazes across the desert, recounting the events that have led them all here. The forty year traveling period was full of shortcomings and iniquities. Moses recounts that he needed help judging the nation and could not do it alone. Moshe reminds the people that they passed through a great and fearsome desert but not without the trial of the spies whose complaints misled and scared the nation causing the generation of the desert to die. Only their children had the opportunity to enter the holy land. “Also against me,” Moshe admits, “God was angry for your sake,” saying, ”you too shall not go in.” Moshe then recounts the nations who would not let us pass through their land. Moshe tells us of his successor Joshua and urges us to not be afraid - Hashem will fight with us.

Rewriting the past and giving rebuke is heard way too often from those in positions of power. When a politicians agenda cannot come to fruition, rather than owning up to it he may rewrite his initial commitment, saying his agenda was different from the beginning. This opens the door to blame shifting and gives a chance for all stakeholders to reconsider the ground on which they stand. This chaotic unhealthy leadership is so directly rooted in self esteem and general approval ratings.

Not so with Moshe. At the time of the spies, Moses said, “the idea was good in my eyes.” Then we were decreed to die in the desert because of how the spies reported the land. Now after the fact, Moses still says, “the idea was good in my eyes.” Isn't this political suicide! We do not see this ownership today because leaders are not invested in the people. In truth, Aa leader’s core job is learning to trust his followers by being honest to them when assessing the road ahead. Politicians who go back on their word forget that admitting that their idea was bad shows conviction, not weakness. While Moshe does take his time to rebuke, it is measured out and well directed. The verse shares that Moses rebuked the Jewish people after he had smitten Sichon, king of the Amorites and Og, king of Bashan. To find conviction in rebuke, the Lubavitcher Rebbe teaches to make a physical offering or to serve the one you wish to rebuke. Joshua, Himself one of the spies, is Moshe’s closest disciple. The talmud says that Moshe is like the sun and Joshua like the moon, absorbing and reflecting Moses’s brilliant rebuke through all hours of the night.

Shabbat Shalom, Avi
