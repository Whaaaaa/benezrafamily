In this week's Torah portion we find a very strange phenomenon. Not unusually, God tells Moshe (and Aaron's son, Elazar) to go count the Jewish people. But unlike every other time they are counted, this time each family is called twice. The verses go through the 45 different families that stemmed from the 12 tribes and calls each one by name like so: “the sons of Shimon, according to their families: to Nemuel, the Nemueli family, to Yamin, the Yamini family, to Yachin, the Yachini family…”

Suffice it to say that the repetitiveness needs explanation. Every single letter in the Torah is there for a reason. There are no extras. So when the Torah repeats 45 family names with no additional information being given over, we must pause.

Fortunately, Rashi tells us what’s going on: The nations of the world where laughing that the Jewish people took such pride in there familial heritage. Tribal affiliation and family heritage travels through the patriarchal line and the nations would tease, “What are the chances that any slave nation could know their fathers?”

Would not the Egyptians sleep with any woman they pleased?

“How is it possible,” they laughed, “that the Jewish people know their fathers’ ancestral heritage?”

Therefore, Rashi says, God places each and every name in between the two letters of the Divine name to testify upon them that they retained the sexual purity that befits a nation of royalty.

This is easier to see in the Hebrew, but each name was called with a Hey before it and a Yud after, the two letter name of God, Y-ah.

An example: “To Yamin, family of HaYamini, the Yaminite.”

The trait we are discussing here is called yesod, or foundation, and actually refers to all relationships and relating between people. (It's not by accident that the Torah uses the term “to know” to refer to relations between a man and wife.)

I think I understand finally this year, why this specific counting of the Jewish people is the one where God places his name on each family. We have been counted many times since we left Egypt but suddenly now this becomes necessary. If you remember, the story that bridges last week's Parsha and this week's, was the one of the Midianite princesses that came promiscuously into the camp to entrap the Jewish people.

Simultaneously, we become aware of the Jewish people engaging in idol worship. Not just any idol, but actually the most disgusting idol we know of. (The service was to defecate in front of it.) Just after the Jewish people begin serving this idol, known as Baal Peor, a plague breaks out in the camp and many of the Jewish people begin to die.

The Nesivos Shalom points out that the plague begins due to idol worship but it only ends after Pinchas, Elazar's son, drives a spear through Zimri, a Jewish tribal leader, and Cozbi, the Midianite princess, whilst they are engaged in an act of sexual immorality before the camp.

We see an interesting and strong value placed on sexual morality here. The sin of being with a midianite woman is a debate among the sages. Some hold that it is only a rabbinic prohibition and others holds that it is a Torah prohibition. But everyone agrees that the Torah prohibition of idol worship falls under the three sins one must give their life not to transgress. And lying with a non-Jewish woman isn't even agreed upon by all to be a Torah prohibition! How could this act of Pinchas which seems so clearly related to the promiscuity that was entering the camp, put an end to a plaque that befell the Jewish people due to idolatry?!

The answer, our sages tell us, is that the Jewish people have an internal knowledge of God that is unshakeable. And according to the kabbalists, when the evil inclination seeks to destroy the relationship between a Jew and God, he knows that he cannot strike at that relationship directly. He must go through the conduit of sins of sexuality. (Rashi actually tells us in last week's Parsha that the Jewish people began worshipping Baal Peor only after the midianite princesses pulled the idols out of their tunics and said they would only lie with the Jewish people if they first served the idol.) When Pinchas called attention the underlying problem, the plague came to an end.

So I think that now, after this story of Pinchas, God seeks to remind the Jewish people that we have pure roots. That we needn't worry what any of the nations say. That we do have an ancestral heritage that is passed through our father's tribal identity. And that just like they figured out how to stay pure and endure the slavery they were faced with, we too can stand in this challenge.

The Arizal says that this is the central challenge of our generation. To work on Yesod, sexual morality and relationships. May we merit to keep ourselves distant from sins, and to relate to one another in a Torah-true and healthy way, and to thereby be in touch with our innate knowledge of the Creator and build an intensely loving relationship with the One!

Good Shabbos!
