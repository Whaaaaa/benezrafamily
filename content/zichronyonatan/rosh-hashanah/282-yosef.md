In the High Holiday prayers, we say “כדלים וכרשים דפקנו דלתיך”, “like the poor and the destitute we knock on Your doors.”

Rabbi Shalom Scwhadron zt”l asks that the word dafaknu used here doesn’t connote a soft rap at the door, but rather a powerful knock – to bang mightily. This would seem to be out of place with the reference to a beggar, who normally knocks lightly out of embarrassment and humility.

Rabbi Schwadron’s answer will change the way you see Rosh Hashanah forever. Yes, a beggar usually knocks softly, but there is one exception. If he is so destitute that he is literally about to die of starvation, when he feels that his life is on the line and he cannot survive without the help of the homeowner, then he doesn’t tap – he bangs down the door!

We must realize that on Rosh Hashanah our lease on life is over. We were given the past year, with all the blessings we enjoyed, but they have zero bearing on tomorrow. Tomorrow is a new year, and everything requires a new judgment and allocation. If we were healthy last year, it has absolutely no reason to continue further. If we had enough to eat, a roof over our heads, and the rest of our basic needs, there is no guarantee for the future. In short, we need to realize that there is no such thing as status quo from before Rosh Hashanah to after Rosh Hashanah.

In this light, we are not just paupers begging for a handout from the Almighty – we are standing at the brink of death and should be banging heaven’s doors down in desperation. The way we knock is with our prayers, with our tears, and with our yearning to go higher.

But the same is true on the side of good. Rosh Hashanah is a time of unlimited possibility. All our constraints and all the tests which stood against us last year are about to expire. If we struggled with our finances last year, there is no reason to assume it must be that way tomorrow. If we suffered from bad health, from difficult relationships, or from any other hardship in the past, that doesn’t lock us in for the coming year. On Rosh Hashanah we have a clean slate which is ready to be filled with untold blessings.

So, let us see things for the way they really are – without taking anything for granted – and truly break open the gates of heaven with our Rosh Hashanah prayers. In this way, may we all merit to be written and sealed in the book of life and to a year of all things good.

Shanah Tovah Umetukah,

Yosef
