This week's Torah Portion, Nitzavim, has a beautiful paragraph referring to where Torah can be found.

It clarifies that it is not a the top of a mountain nor at the bottom of the sea; it is actually very close. Torah exists in our hearts and minds.

To put in perspective how revolutionary this concept is, I'd like to point out that until not very long ago, everything took a lot of work. Fields had to be plowed to allow room for the seeds to be planted. Water had to be drawn from wells and fetched from streams and rivers. Everything necessary for physical survival was very difficult and took a lot of time and energy.

After exerting so much just to sustain daily life, it's very plausible that one might say that he has no time left to focus on spirituality. Only when all of his needs are taken care of, he may begin to connect to Hashem.

But we all know how that goes. The day never grows more hours and our workloads only increase as we age. We may face the terrifying possibility that years have passed and our souls are woefully undeveloped.

So Hashem tells us: do your work. Tend to your fields. Don't delay connecting and praying, because you can do both at once.

Follow the laws of gardening, keep your thoughts pure and holy, pray while you work. Don't separate the spiritual and the physical because you live in a world that is made of up of both and your body itself is the meeting of these two realms.

Our Rabbis teach us that if you do holy things even while you need to be in a mundane world, even the mundane will become holy.

Uncle Yonatan was a living example of this. He constantly had conversations about science that morphed into questions about Jewish Law. He took walks with prayers on his lips. He found a way to make every aspect of his life elevated, and we know that in Heaven he is enjoying an elevated place near The Holy Throne of Hashem.
