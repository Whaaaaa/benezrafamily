During the Great Flood, the entire world was destroyed. Every person, every animal, every building – everything except those housed within Noach's ark. When Noach finally opened the door of the ark, after a full year inside, he stepped out onto the dry ground of a new world and he was filled with an overwhelming sense of gratitude. Gratitude for the precious gift of life and for the priceless opportunity to walk the face of Hashem's beautiful world. As an expression of thanks, Noach built an altar and brought an offering to Hashem from all of the kosher animals.

I can't tell you exactly why, but this offering elicited a great amount of satisfaction from Hashem. Maybe it was the level of gratitude with which it was fueled, maybe it was because it marked a drastic change from the downward spiral the world had been following up until that point, but whatever the reason it gave Hashem great pleasure.

The Torah tells us that at that moment a very great thing happened – Hashem swore that he would never again destroy the world in its entirety. No matter how low the world would sink, no matter how deep mankind would trudge into the quagmire of iniquity, the world would not be destroyed. What a monumental accomplishment Noach had achieved! Who knows how many times this promise has saved the world from destruction? Who knows how heavily all of mankind depends on Noach's offering that day?

Yet the Ramban comments that, at the time, no man was aware of the great waves being made in heaven. Hashem swore, but he did not communicate His promise to Noach or to anyone else. Finally, thousands of years later, when Moshe wrote the Torah, Hashem revealed to him what had taken place upstairs as Noach brought his offering, and that is how we know about it today.

For me, this is wildly encouraging. Noach had just won a victory for the ages, he had just made one of the greatest accomplishments in the history of mankind, yet he never knew about the fruits of his labor.

Sometimes we put our heart and soul into achieving some greatness of spirit. It could be a pure and heartfelt prayer, a mitzvah which we struggle to fulfill to completion, or an insult which we manage to swallow without lashing back. It could be a kind act or a kind word or a million other things. And we wonder if we really made a difference in the world. Usually we'll never find out, but let's remember the offering of Noach and realize that we very well might have just scored a point which can sustain the whole world, which can bring Moshiach closer, which can elicit great pleasure from Hashem. We may not know about it, but we're no worse off than Noach, whose offering sustains us in every generation without his knowledge.

Just keep striving for greatness. You never know what you may be achieving.

Good Shabbos,

Yosef
