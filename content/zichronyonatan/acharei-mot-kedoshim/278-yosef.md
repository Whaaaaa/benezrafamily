The Torah exhorts us 'Do not follow after idolatrous trinkets and do not make for yourselves molten gods.' At first glance, the verse seems redundant, but Rashi reveals the great lesson which is being conveyed. He states, 'At first they are nothing but worthless trinkets, but, if you follow them, they will end up being gods over you.'

Rabbi Yeruchom Levovitz explains this to be an all encompassing lesson, not limited to idolatry alone. There are a great many things in life which, in truth, are useless, powerless, and devoid of any importance. But if, for whatever reason, we decide to make these things a part of our lives, we very quickly become wholly dependent upon them.

Rabbi Levovitz cites a case from the Gemara concerning the first king of our nation, Shaul. When the prophet Shmuel was sent by Hashem to anoint Shaul as king, the humble Shaul fled, wanting nothing of the power and prestige of the throne. Still, when it was later decreed for Shaul to step down from the throne in deference to Dovid, he fought mightily to kill Dovid and retain his kingship. This is but one example of how honor, once despised and unwanted, could take of hold of even the greatest men, eventually ruling over them.

The same can be said of countless physical pleasures and conveniences. How many times have we scorned the overindulgence of friends and neighbors, only to later once indulge ourselves and become utterly dependent on what we formerly considered absurd?

The same can be said of many helpers and devices. One may be fine in school on their own, but once they find some brilliant friend who is ready to help, they suddenly find that they just can't keep up on their own.

Or how about the backup camera which I never missed until I got one – and now I'm handicapped without it.

I don't mean to say that every convenience is an evil, but we should at least be aware that every silly thing we let into our lives will, almost necessarily, become an imperative.

So let's think twice before we get involved in things we don't need, especially if they are out of our reach. And as for those things which already have taken hold of us, let us remember their true lack of value and realize that it is only by our decision that they gained mastery over us.

Good Shabbos,

Yosef
